The Geocentric

Earth at the centre. Each body sits at its compass bearing from the station and its distance from Earth, on a log scale. Dimmed and dashed when below the horizon; the tail behind each dot is its last hour.

The Sky Overhead

Looking up from the station: the zenith at the centre, the horizon at the rim, north at the top and east to the left. Stars to magnitude 5.5 with the constellation figures; the dashed line is the ecliptic, the Sun's path.

The Next Visible Pass

The sky as it will stand at the highest point of the soonest satellite pass you can see. The bold stretch of the arc is where it will be visible; the faint ends are too low, in daylight, or in Earth's shadow. During the pass the satellite moves along the arc live, hollow once it enters the shadow.

Positions computed on the station with Skyfield and the JPL DE421 ephemeris. Meteor shower peaks from the IMO calendar. Satellite orbits from CelesTrak, SatNOGS or the TLE API; comet orbits from the Minor Planet Center. Star catalogue and constellation figures from Olaf Frohn's d3-celestial (BSD licence).
