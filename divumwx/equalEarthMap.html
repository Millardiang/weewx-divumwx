Wrap blends the Equal Earth projection (Šavrič, Jenny & Patterson, 2018) into an orthographic view of a sphere: at 0% every country has its true relative area, and at 100% you see one hemisphere as it would look from space. Auto view keeps this station, and the Sun and Moon whenever they are above the station's horizon, on the near side of the globe. Dragging, spinning, the arrow keys or the Face buttons switch it off; press Auto view to return. Shading marks night and the civil, nautical and astronomical twilight bands. The Sun and Moon tracks trace the points they pass directly over for one full trip round the Earth: 24 hours for the Sun and about 24 hours 50 minutes for the Moon, which moves on in its orbit. There is a dot for each hour. The time slider moves the Sun and Moon up to 12 hours either side of now. Everything updates every 5 seconds. Positions use low-precision almanac formulae, accurate to about 0.01° for the Sun and 0.3° for the Moon. Boundaries: Natural Earth 1:110m.
