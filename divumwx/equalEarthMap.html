Wrap blends the Equal Earth projection (Šavrič, Jenny & Patterson, 2018) into an orthographic view of a sphere: at 0% every country has its true relative area, and at 100% you see one hemisphere as it would look from space. Auto view keeps this station, and the Sun and Moon whenever they are above the station's horizon, on the near side of the globe. Dragging, spinning, the arrow keys or the Face buttons switch it off; press Auto view to return. Shading marks night and the civil, nautical and astronomical twilight bands. The Sun and Moon tracks trace the points they pass directly over for one full trip round the Earth: 24 hours for the Sun and about 24 hours 50 minutes for the Moon, which moves on in its orbit. There is a dot for each hour. The time slider moves the Sun and Moon up to 12 hours either side of now. On the flat map, click a country for its true area and its size on the map compared with the same area at the equator: it reads ×1.00 everywhere on Equal Earth, while Mercator inflates high latitudes (Greenland about ×16, Russia about ×5) and Antarctica, which reaches the pole, without limit. Tissot circles are all 500 km in radius on the ground: Equal Earth keeps their area and stretches their shape toward the edges, Mercator keeps them round and lets them grow. Areas are computed on a sphere of radius 6,371 km. Everything updates every 5 seconds. Positions use low-precision almanac formulae, accurate to about 0.01° for the Sun and 0.3° for the Moon. Boundaries: Natural Earth 1:110m.
