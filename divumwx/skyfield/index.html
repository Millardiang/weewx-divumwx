The Sky Now

Looking up from the station: the zenith at the centre, the horizon at the rim, north at the top and east to the left, as on a star chart held overhead. When the Sun is up the stars are dimmed, standing where they are behind the daylight. Hover or tap a mark for its position.

Rise & Set · today

Each bar spans the time the body is above the horizon today; the tick across it is when it crosses the meridian, highest in the sky. The background shades are today's daylight, civil, nautical and astronomical twilight, and night. The vertical line is now.

The Sun's Path · today

The Sun's altitude against its compass bearing from midnight to midnight, a dot every hour; the Moon's path is dashed. Below the horizon line, the bands are civil, nautical and astronomical twilight depth. The ringed marks are where they are now.

The Solar System · today

Each planet at its heliocentric longitude, seen from above the Sun's north pole; orbits are spaced logarithmically so the inner planets aren't lost. The dashed ray marks 0°, the direction of the March equinox. Comets are diamonds.

The Solar Year · daylight day by day

Sunrise and sunset (solid) and solar noon (dashed) for every day of the year, over the same daylight and twilight shades as above, in local clock time — so the daylight-saving steps in spring and autumn are real. The highlighted line is today; hover for any day.

The Equation of Time

Sundial time minus clock time at local standard noon, day by day: above the line the sundial runs ahead. Its two humps come from Earth's tilt and its elliptical orbit — the same pair that draws the analemma's figure-eight.

The Analemma · the Sun at noon

Where the Sun stands at local standard noon, week by week through the year: higher in summer, and east or west of south as the sundial runs ahead or behind. The ringed point is today.

The Lunar Month · this lunation

New moon to new moon in thirty steps, with the principal phases dated; today's disc is ringed. Hover or tap any disc for its date and illumination.
