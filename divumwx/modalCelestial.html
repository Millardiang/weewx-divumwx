Guide — Kp-Index

The Kp-Index is a good indicator of the likelihood of viewing the Aurora Borealis (Northern Lights) — the higher the figure, the greater the probability.

Guide — A-Index

The A-Index indicates possible enhanced VHF radio propagation at ranges up to 1000 miles or more. During strong activity, frequencies from 28–433MHz (or higher) can support communication at mid to high latitudes.

Radio Ham Guide

Aurora communications can be used by VHF enthusiasts via aurora scatter propagation, using specialised operating techniques to reach distances of 2000km or more on 28/50/144/433MHz.

Viewing Guide

Meteors are best viewed during the night, though they can enter the atmosphere at any time of day — just harder to see except around dawn and dusk. Nearby ambient light and moonlight can make viewing difficult; showers are best seen away from city lights.

Radio Ham Guide

Meteor scatter communications can be used by VHF enthusiasts and commercial operators alike, using specialised techniques to reach distances of 2000km or more on VHF frequencies.

Moon Facts — Did You Know?
  • The Moon formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago.
  • High and low tides are caused by the Moon's gravitational pull.
  • The Moon orbits the Earth roughly every 29.53 days.
  • Sunlit surface temperatures can reach 260°F (127°C).
  • On the dark side, temperatures can drop to −280°F (−173°C).

Radio Ham Guide (EME)

Earth–Moon–Earth (EME), also known as Moon bounce, is a technique that relies on radio waves reflecting off the Moon's surface between two Earth-based stations, using VHF and UHF amateur radio bands.
